\begin{abstract}
For a finite confusability graph $G$ and an order $\alpha\in[0,2]$, we
minimize the Petz--R\'enyi information radius over all classical--quantum
channels whose output supports respect the nonedges of $G$.  We prove that
the resulting parameter $\vtheta_\alpha$ is additive under disjoint union,
multiplicative under strong product, normalized on the one-vertex graph, and
monotone under entanglement-assisted cohomomorphisms.  Thus every
$\vtheta_\alpha$ is an element of the entanglement-assisted asymptotic
spectrum of graphs introduced by Li and Zuiddam.  The proof of
multiplicativity applies to arbitrary joint representations and is based on a
nested-compression identity, not on an assumption that an optimizer factors.

The pentagon distinguishes infinitely many members of the family.  More
precisely, for $0\leq\alpha\leq 1/2$ we obtain explicit two-sided bounds, and
$\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)=5/2$ for $1/2\leq\alpha\leq2$.  A sparse sequence of
orders gives infinitely many pairwise distinct spectral points, all distinct
from the Lov\'asz number.  At the endpoints,
$\vtheta_0=\vtheta$, $\vtheta_1=2^{C_{\min}}$, and
$\vtheta_2=\Sigma$.  Finally, an exact rational moment certificate gives a
32-vertex graph for which $C_{\min}(G)<\log_2\Sigma(G)$, so the last two
endpoints are distinct as graph parameters.  The finite certificate is
included with a deterministic verifier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The asymptotic spectrum of graphs packages graph parameters that are
simultaneously additive under disjoint union, multiplicative under strong
product, normalized, and monotone for an appropriate cohomomorphism preorder.
Zuiddam used this framework for ordinary graph homomorphisms
\cite{Zuiddam2019}; Li and Zuiddam introduced its quantum and
entanglement-assisted variants \cite{LiZuiddam2020}.  In the strongest of
these preorders, Li and Zuiddam proved that the Lov\'asz number is a
spectral point, reported no other examples, and asked whether it might even
be the only point \cite[Theorem~18 and the discussion following
Conjecture~19]{LiZuiddam2020}.  To our knowledge, the present construction is
the first to produce points of the entanglement-assisted graph spectrum
beyond the Lov\'asz number, and hence the first infinite family in that
spectrum.

R\'enyi radii have appeared previously in graph theory from several
directions.  Dalai used classical--quantum R\'enyi information radii to
recover the Lov\'asz bound as the zero-error endpoint of a sphere-packing
construction \cite{Dalai2013}; this does not give finite-order graph
parameters in an asymptotic spectrum.  Vrana showed that each already-known
point of the ordinary graph spectrum admits a distribution-valued
probabilistic refinement \cite{Vrana2020}; that construction presupposes a
scalar spectral point and concerns the ordinary cohomomorphism preorder.  In
the weaker quantum and commuting-quantum preorders, fractional-Haemers and
tracial-Haemers type parameters provide further points
\cite{LiZuiddam2020,GaoGriblingLi2022}.  Li and Zuiddam noted that the
corresponding monotonicity was not known for their stronger
entanglement-assisted preorder.  The result here proves both multiplicativity
for arbitrary joint representations and monotonicity for that stronger
preorder.  We do not claim that this family exhausts the spectrum.

We construct a one-parameter family of points.  Let $(\rho_x)_{x\in V(G)}$
be density operators such that $\rho_x\rho_y=0$ whenever $x\neq y$ are
nonadjacent in the confusability graph $G$.  For the Petz--R\'enyi divergence
$D_\alpha$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def-parameter}
 \log_2\vtheta_\alpha(G)
 =\inf_{(\rho_x),\,\sigma>0}\max_x D_\alpha(\rho_x\Vert\sigma),
 \qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq2.
\end{equation}
The output dimension is arbitrary and finite, and no attainment is assumed.
Our first main result is the following.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral-point theorem]\label{thm:spectrum}
For every $\alpha\in[0,2]$, the map $G\mapsto\vtheta_\alpha(G)$ belongs to
Li and Zuiddam's entanglement-assisted asymptotic spectrum
$X(\cG,\leq_*)$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem combines three different mechanisms.  Supported-state
minimization converts \eqref{eq:def-parameter} into a projection-packing
formula involving the power compression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compression-intro}
 c_s(P;\sigma)=\Tr_{\ran P}(P\sigma^sP)^{1/s},
 \qquad s=1-\alpha,
\end{equation}
with the logarithmic limit at $s=0$.  Nested compression gives an exact tower
identity.  Applied to the row spans of an arbitrary representation of a
strong product, it proves the difficult direction of multiplicativity.  A
separate orthogonal-sum inequality proves disjoint-union additivity.  Finally,
a flagged-mixture argument and data processing prove monotonicity for
entanglement-assisted cohomomorphisms.  The last step is the reason for the
range $\alpha\leq2$.

The family is not a redundant parametrization.  On the pentagon we prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pentagon-intro}
 \sqrt5\left(\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\right)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}
 \leq \vtheta_\alpha(C_5)
 \leq \min\left\{\frac52,5^{1/(2-2\alpha)}\right\}
 \quad(0\leq\alpha\leq\tfrac12),
\end{equation}
and equality $\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)=5/2$ on $[1/2,2]$.  Interpolation between
trace-norm and Hilbert--Schmidt operator inequalities is the main ingredient.
The bounds imply the following.

\begin{theorem}[Infinitely many points]\label{thm:infinite}
For $k\geq0$, put $\alpha_k=(8^{k+1}+1)^{-1}$.  The points
$\vtheta$, $\vtheta_{\alpha_0},\vtheta_{\alpha_1},\ldots$ are pairwise
distinct elements of $X(\cG,\leq_*)$.
\end{theorem}

The endpoints have operational interpretations.  We recover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoints-intro}
 \vtheta_0=\vtheta,\qquad
 \vtheta_1=2^{C_{\min}},\qquad
 \vtheta_2=\Sigma,
\end{equation}
where $C_{\min}$ is the smallest Holevo capacity of a compatible
classical--quantum channel and $\Sigma$ is the fractional common-dominator
simulation cost of Duan and Winter \cite{DuanWinter2016}.  The first two
identities are information-radius formulas; the last uses the equivalence of
supported Petz order two and supported max-relative entropy.  Duan and Winter
already proved multiplicativity of $\Sigma$ for two fixed cq-graphs
\cite[Proposition~18]{DuanWinter2016}.  The graph-level product statement
needed here is stronger in quantifiers: a representation of $G\boxtimes H$
may be chosen jointly and need not factor through representations of the two
graphs.  The nested-compression argument handles precisely this issue.

To show that the last two endpoints are themselves different, we give a
finite exact example.

\begin{theorem}[A small endpoint separation]\label{thm:small-graph}
There is an explicitly defined 32-vertex graph $G_{32}$ such that
\begin{equation}
 2^{C_{\min}(G_{32})}\leq 6.186983\ldots
 < 6.194232\ldots\leq\Sigma(G_{32}).
\end{equation}
Both outer bounds are certified by exact rational data included with this
paper.  In particular, $\vtheta_1\neq\vtheta_2$ as graph parameters.
\end{theorem}

The lower bound on $\Sigma$ comes from a dimension-independent moment
relaxation.  Its key observation is that the harmonic compression is a
vector-state moment of a second commuting family of projections.  The dual
matrix is rational and its positive semidefiniteness is certified exactly by
an integer factor plus a diagonally dominant integer residual.  A numerical
SDP solver was used to find the certificate but is not used to accept it.

We emphasize the limits of the paper.  The new points all dominate the
Lov\'asz number, so they do not improve the Lov\'asz upper bound on
entanglement-assisted Shannon capacity and do not decide whether the Lov\'asz
number is the least spectral point.  Orders above two, the exact pentagon
curve on $(0,1/2)$, and a classification of the spectrum remain open.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

All graphs are finite and simple.  They are interpreted as confusability
graphs: distinct nonadjacent vertices must be represented by orthogonal
states.  The disjoint union is denoted $G\sqcup H$ and the strong product by
$G\boxt H$.  We set $\vtheta_\alpha(K_0)=0$ for the empty graph.

\subsection{Compatible states and R\'enyi radii}

\begin{definition}
A family $(\rho_x)_{x\in V(G)}$ of density operators on a finite-dimensional
Hilbert space is \emph{compatible with $G$} if
$\rho_x\rho_y=0$ whenever $x\neq y$ are nonadjacent in $G$.
\end{definition}

For a faithful density operator $\sigma$, the base-two Petz--R\'enyi
divergence is
\begin{equation}
 D_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 =\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log_2\Tr\rho^\alpha\sigma^{1-\alpha}
 \quad(\alpha\neq1),
\end{equation}
with the continuous value $D(\rho\Vert\sigma)$ at $\alpha=1$ and
$D_0(\rho\Vert\sigma)=-\log_2\Tr\Pi_\rho\sigma$ at $\alpha=0$.
Definition \eqref{eq:def-parameter} is unchanged if centers are allowed to be
singular with the standard support convention: a singular center can be
mixed with the maximally mixed state and the mixing parameter sent to zero.

The graph parameters $C_{\min}$ and $\Sigma$ can be defined independently as
\begin{align}
 C_{\min}(G)
 &=\inf_{(\rho_x)\text{ compatible}}\max_p
    \left[S\!\left(\sum_xp_x\rho_x\right)-\sum_xp_xS(\rho_x)\right],
 \label{eq:cmin-def}\\
 \Sigma(G)
 &=\inf\left\{\Tr T:T\geq\rho_x\ \forall x,
       \ (\rho_x)\text{ compatible}\right\}.
 \label{eq:sigma-def}
\end{align}
The infima range over arbitrary finite output dimensions.

\subsection{The entanglement-assisted preorder}

We use the complement convention of Li and Zuiddam
\cite{LiZuiddam2020}.  In confusability language, $G\leq_*H$ means that
there are a nonzero positive semidefinite operator $\omega$ and operators
$\omega_g^h\geq0$ satisfying
\begin{align}
 \sum_{h\in V(H)}\omega_g^h&=\omega &&\text{for every }g,\label{eq:ea-sum}\\
 \omega_g^h\omega_{g'}^{h'}&=0
 &&\begin{array}{l}
   \text{if $g\neq g'$ are nonadjacent in $G$, and}\\[-1mm]
   \text{$h=h'$ or $h\sim_Hh'$.}
 \end{array}\label{eq:ea-orth}
\end{align}
We normalize $\Tr\omega=1$ and work on $\supp\omega$.

The entanglement-assisted asymptotic spectrum $X(\cG,\leq_*)$ consists of
maps $\phi$ from finite graphs to $[0,\infty)$ satisfying
\begin{align*}
 \phi(G\sqcup H)&=\phi(G)+\phi(H),&
 \phi(G\boxt H)&=\phi(G)\phi(H),\\
 \phi(K_1)&=1,&
 G\leq_*H&\Longrightarrow\phi(G)\leq\phi(H).
\end{align*}

\section{Supported minimization and power compressions}\label{sec:compression}

Let $P\neq0$ be a projection and let $\sigma>0$.  For
$s\in[-1,1]\setminus\{0\}$ define, on $\ran P$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ks}
 K_s(\sigma,P)=(P\sigma^sP)^{1/s},\qquad
 Z_s(\sigma,P)=\Tr K_s(\sigma,P),
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K0}
 K_0(\sigma,P)=\exp(P(\ln\sigma)P),\qquad
 Z_0(\sigma,P)=\Tr K_0(\sigma,P).
\end{equation}
The operators are extended by zero off $\ran P$.

\begin{proposition}[Supported-state variational formula]\label{prop:supported}
For $0\leq\alpha\leq2$, $s=1-\alpha$, and $P\neq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supported-var}
 \min_{\substack{\rho\text{ state}\\\supp\rho\subseteq\ran P}}
 D_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)=-\log_2 Z_s(\sigma,P).
\end{equation}
The minimum is attained at $K_s/Z_s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $0<\alpha<1$, put $A=P\sigma^{1-\alpha}P$.  Schatten H\"older gives
\[
 \Tr\rho^\alpha A
 \leq\bigl(\Tr A^{1/(1-\alpha)}\bigr)^{1-\alpha}.
\]
Because $(\alpha-1)^{-1}<0$, this is the required lower bound on the
divergence, and equality holds for
$\rho=A^{1/(1-\alpha)}/\Tr A^{1/(1-\alpha)}$.

For $1<\alpha\leq2$, diagonalize $A=P\sigma^{1-\alpha}P$ on $\ran P$.
Scalar Jensen applied to the diagonal entries of $\rho^\alpha$, followed by
the scalar constrained minimum, gives
\[
 \Tr A\rho^\alpha
 \geq\bigl(\Tr A^{1/(1-\alpha)}\bigr)^{1-\alpha},
\]
with equality at the same state.  This is also the reverse-H\"older
calculation.  At $\alpha=1$, the Gibbs variational identity gives
\[
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 =D\!\left(\rho\middle\Vert\frac{K_0}{Z_0}\right)-\log_2 Z_0.
\]
At $\alpha=0$, maximizing $\Tr\Pi_\rho\sigma$ over states supported in
$P$ gives $\Tr P\sigma=Z_1$.  This proves all cases.
\end{proof}

Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packing-formula}
 \frac{1}{\vtheta_\alpha(G)}
 =\sup_{(P_x),\,\sigma>0}\min_x Z_{1-\alpha}(\sigma,P_x),
\end{equation}
where $(P_x)$ ranges over projection packings of $G$, with
$P_xP_y=0$ at distinct nonedges.  Indeed, the supports of every compatible
family form such a packing, while the minimizing states in
\cref{prop:supported} form a compatible family for every packing.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint identities]\label{prop:endpoints}
The parameters in \eqref{eq:def-parameter} satisfy
\[
 \vtheta_0=\vtheta,\qquad
 \vtheta_1=2^{C_{\min}},\qquad
 \vtheta_2=\Sigma.
\]
Moreover, $\alpha\mapsto\vtheta_\alpha(G)$ is nondecreasing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At order zero, \eqref{eq:packing-formula} is the standard projector form of
the Lov\'asz number \cite{Lovasz1979,Dalai2013}.  At order one, the
information-radius identity
\[
 \max_p\chi(p;\rho)=\inf_\sigma\max_xD(\rho_x\Vert\sigma)
\]
and \eqref{eq:cmin-def} give the second equality.  At order two,
$K_{-1}=(P\sigma^{-1}P)^{-1}$ is the shorted operator
\cite{AndersonTrapp1975}.  The same supported
minimum is obtained with max-relative entropy.  Normalizing a common
dominator $T=t\sigma$ identifies the optimized max-radius with
$\log_2\Sigma(G)$, giving the third equality.  Monotonicity follows from
monotonicity of the Petz divergence in its order before taking infima.
\end{proof}

The key algebraic fact is the following exact nesting property.

\begin{lemma}[Tower identity]\label{lem:tower}
If $P\leq Q$ are projections and $s\in[-1,1]$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tower-op}
 K_s(K_s(\sigma,Q),P)=K_s(\sigma,P).
\end{equation}
If $z=Z_s(\sigma,Q)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tower-normalized}
 Z_s\!\left(\frac{K_s(\sigma,Q)}{z},P\right)
 =\frac{Z_s(\sigma,P)}{z}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $s\neq0$, the $s$th power of $K_s(\sigma,Q)$ inside $\ran Q$ is
$Q\sigma^sQ$.  Compression to $P\leq Q$ gives
$P\sigma^sP$, proving \eqref{eq:tower-op}.  For $s=0$, take logarithms
inside $\ran Q$.  Equation \eqref{eq:tower-normalized} follows from
degree-one homogeneity in the first argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Strong-product multiplicativity}\label{sec:product}

The easy product inequality follows by tensoring near-optimal witnesses.
The reverse inequality must allow a representation of $G\boxt H$ that is not
a tensor product.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary-joint product theorem]\label{thm:product}
For $0\leq\alpha\leq2$ and finite graphs $G,H$,
\begin{equation}
 \vtheta_\alpha(G\boxt H)
 =\vtheta_\alpha(G)\vtheta_\alpha(H).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $s=1-\alpha$ and take a joint packing $(P_{xy})$ of $G\boxt H$ with a
faithful center $\sigma$.  Suppose
$Z_s(\sigma,P_{xy})\geq2^{-r}$ for every pair $(x,y)$.  For each $x$, let
$S_x$ be the projection onto the span of the ranges of $(P_{xy})_y$ and put
$z_x=Z_s(\sigma,S_x)$.

If $x,x'$ are distinct nonadjacent vertices of $G$, then every
$(x,y),(x',y')$ is a nonedge of the strong product.  Hence $S_xS_{x'}=0$,
so $(S_x)$ is a packing of $G$.  For each fixed $x$, $(P_{xy})_y$ is a
packing of $H$ on $\ran S_x$.  By \eqref{eq:tower-normalized}, its reduced
center $K_s(\sigma,S_x)/z_x$ satisfies
\[
 Z_s\!\left(\frac{K_s(\sigma,S_x)}{z_x},P_{xy}\right)
 =\frac{Z_s(\sigma,P_{xy})}{z_x}
 \geq\frac{2^{-r}}{z_x}.
\]
The packing formula for $H$ therefore implies
$\vtheta_\alpha(H)\leq2^rz_x$, or
$z_x\geq2^{\log_2\vtheta_\alpha(H)-r}$.
Applying the packing formula once more to $(S_x)$ gives
\[
 \log_2\vtheta_\alpha(G)
 \leq r-\log_2\vtheta_\alpha(H).
\]
Thus every joint radius $r$ is at least the sum of the two graph radii.
Taking the infimum proves the reverse inequality.

For the forward inequality, tensor packings and centers.  The traces in
\eqref{eq:Ks} multiply under tensor products, so near-optimal witnesses give
the product upper bound.  No attainment is required.
\end{proof}

At $\alpha=2$, \cref{thm:product} gives
$\Sigma(G\boxt H)=\Sigma(G)\Sigma(H)$.  This removes the regularization from
the graph-level no-signalling simulation quantity considered by Duan and
Winter \cite{DuanWinter2016}; their fixed-channel multiplicativity does not
by itself imply the graph-level statement because the optimizing joint
representation need not factor.

\section{Disjoint-union additivity}\label{sec:union}

We first record the compression identity in divergence form.

\begin{lemma}[Compression identity]\label{lem:compression}
Let $Q\neq0$ be a projection, $s=1-\alpha\in[-1,1]$, and
$\sigma_Q=K_s(\sigma,Q)/Z_s(\sigma,Q)$.  Every state $\rho$ supported in
$Q$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compression-div}
 D_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 =D_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma_Q)-\log_2 Z_s(\sigma,Q).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\alpha\notin\{0,1\}$, insert
$Q\sigma^sQ=Z_s^s\sigma_Q^s$ into the Petz moment.  At $\alpha=0$ use
$Q\sigma Q=Z_1\sigma_Q$ under the support projection of $\rho$.  At
$\alpha=1$, use
$Q(\ln\sigma)Q=\ln\sigma_Q+(\ln Z_0)Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Orthogonal-sum inequality]\label{lem:orthogonal-sum}
If $Q_1,\ldots,Q_m$ are mutually orthogonal projections, $\sigma>0$, and
$s\leq1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orthogonal-sum}
 \sum_j Z_s(\sigma,Q_j)\leq1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $s\neq0$, set $A=\sigma^s$ and $f(t)=t^{1/s}$; for $s=0$, set
$A=\ln\sigma$ and $f(t)=e^t$.  In every case used here, $f$ is nonnegative
and convex on the spectrum of $A$, and $\Tr f(A)=1$.  Diagonalize each
$Q_jAQ_j$ in an orthonormal basis $(e_{jk})_k$ of $\ran Q_j$.  Scalar Jensen
for the spectral measure of $A$ in $e_{jk}$ gives
\[
 f(\langle e_{jk},Ae_{jk}\rangle)
 \leq\langle e_{jk},f(A)e_{jk}\rangle.
\]
Summing over the orthonormal family $(e_{jk})_{j,k}$ proves
\eqref{eq:orthogonal-sum}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Disjoint-union additivity]\label{thm:union}
For $0\leq\alpha\leq2$,
\[
 \vtheta_\alpha(G\sqcup H)=\vtheta_\alpha(G)+\vtheta_\alpha(H).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the upper bound, take near-optimal families on orthogonal Hilbert-space
summands with centers $\sigma_G,\sigma_H$ and radius bounds $\log_2a$ and
$\log_2b$.  The block center
$(a\sigma_G\oplus b\sigma_H)/(a+b)$ has radius at most $\log_2(a+b)$ by
\cref{lem:compression}.

Conversely, take a compatible family of $G\sqcup H$ with center $\sigma$ and
radius at most $\lambda$.  Let $Q_G,Q_H$ be the support projections of the
two subfamilies.  Cross pairs are nonedges, so $Q_GQ_H=0$.  By
\cref{lem:compression},
\[
 \vtheta_\alpha(G)\leq2^\lambda Z_s(\sigma,Q_G),\qquad
 \vtheta_\alpha(H)\leq2^\lambda Z_s(\sigma,Q_H).
\]
The sum is at most $2^\lambda$ by \cref{lem:orthogonal-sum}.  Infimize over
$\lambda$.
\end{proof}

In particular, $\vtheta_\alpha(\overline{K_N})=N$ and
$\vtheta_\alpha(K_1)=1$.

\section{Entanglement-assisted monotonicity}\label{sec:ea}

\begin{lemma}[Flagged mixtures]\label{lem:flagged}
Let $\omega_h\geq0$ with $\sum_h\omega_h=\omega$ and $\Tr\omega=1$.
Let $\tau_h$ be states and let $\sigma>0$.  If
$D_\alpha(\tau_h\Vert\sigma)\leq\lambda$ whenever $\omega_h\neq0$, then,
for $0\leq\alpha\leq2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flagged}
 D_\alpha\!\left(\sum_h\omega_h\otimes\tau_h
       \middle\Vert\omega\otimes\sigma\right)\leq\lambda.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $w_h=\Tr\omega_h$ and $\widehat\omega_h=\omega_h/w_h$, omitting zero
terms.  Add a classical flag and consider
\begin{align*}
 \Omega&=\sum_hw_h\proj h\otimes\widehat\omega_h\otimes\tau_h,\\
 \Omega'&=\sum_hw_h\proj h\otimes\widehat\omega_h\otimes\sigma.
\end{align*}
The Petz moment is block additive, and the common first two tensor factors
cancel.  For $0<\alpha<1$ each block moment is at least
$2^{(\alpha-1)\lambda}$; for $1<\alpha\leq2$ the inequalities reverse.
The order-one expression is the weighted average of the relative
entropies, and the order-zero expression is handled by support projections.
Thus $D_\alpha(\Omega\Vert\Omega')\leq\lambda$.

Discarding the flag gives \eqref{eq:flagged}.  Petz data processing holds for
$\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2]$ \cite{Petz1986,Tomamichel2016}; order one follows by
continuity.  At order zero,
$\Pi_\Omega\leq I\otimes\Pi_{\Tr_F\Omega}$ implies directly that the
order-zero divergence cannot increase under the partial trace.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Entanglement-assisted monotonicity]\label{thm:ea-mono}
If $G\leq_*H$, then
$\vtheta_\alpha(G)\leq\vtheta_\alpha(H)$ for every
$\alpha\in[0,2]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take the data $\omega,\omega_g^h$ from
\eqref{eq:ea-sum}--\eqref{eq:ea-orth}, and a compatible target family
$(\tau_h)$ with faithful center $\sigma$.  Define
\[
 \tau'_g=\sum_h\omega_g^h\otimes\tau_h,\qquad
 \sigma'=\omega\otimes\sigma
\]
on $\supp\omega\otimes\cH$.  The states are normalized.  If $g,g'$ are
distinct nonadjacent vertices, then every summand of
$\tau'_g\tau'_{g'}$ vanishes: condition \eqref{eq:ea-orth} kills the terms
with $h=h'$ or $h\sim_Hh'$, while target compatibility kills the remaining
terms.  Thus $(\tau'_g)$ is compatible with $G$.  Applying
\cref{lem:flagged} for each $g$ shows that its radius is at most the target
radius.  Take infima.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:spectrum}]
Normalization is the one-vertex case of \cref{thm:union}; additivity is
\cref{thm:union}; multiplicativity is \cref{thm:product}; and monotonicity is
\cref{thm:ea-mono}.
\end{proof}

The data-processing step above generally fails for Petz orders above two,
and a direct finite flagged-mixture counterexample exists at order three.
Accordingly, the present argument makes no spectral claim for
$\alpha>2$.

\section{The pentagon and infinitely many points}\label{sec:pentagon}

Index $C_5$ by $\mathbb Z_5$ with $i\sim i\pm1$.  In our confusability
convention, a pentagon packing satisfies $P_iP_{i+2}=0$.

\begin{lemma}[Two endpoint operator inequalities]\label{lem:pentagon-endpoints}
For every pentagon packing and every operator $X$,
\begin{align}
 \sum_i\lVert P_iXP_i\rVert_1&\leq\sqrt5\,\lVert X\rVert_1,
 \label{eq:trace-endpoint}\\
 \sum_i\lVert P_iXP_i\rVert_2^2&\leq2\lVert X\rVert_2^2.
 \label{eq:hs-endpoint}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a rank-one operator $X=ab^*$,
$\lVert P_iab^*P_i\rVert_1=\lVert P_ia\rVert\lVert P_ib\rVert$.
The projector form of $\vtheta(C_5)=\sqrt5$ gives
$\sum_i\lVert P_iv\rVert^2\leq\sqrt5\lVert v\rVert^2$; Cauchy--Schwarz
therefore proves \eqref{eq:trace-endpoint} for rank one.  A singular-value
decomposition and the triangle inequality prove it for general $X$.

For \eqref{eq:hs-endpoint}, put $T=\sum_iP_i$.  In the convention
$P_iP_{i+2}=0$, the two surviving projections $P_{i-1}$ and $P_{i+1}$ are
orthogonal.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PTP}
 P_iTP_i=P_i+P_i(P_{i-1}+P_{i+1})P_i\leq2P_i.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
 \mathcal A:\bigoplus_i P_i\mathcal B_2P_i\longrightarrow\mathcal B_2,
 \qquad \mathcal A((X_i)_i)=\sum_iX_i.
\]
For $X_i=P_iX_iP_i$, Cauchy--Schwarz and $ab\leq(a^2+b^2)/2$ give
\[
 |\Tr X_i^*X_j|
 \leq\tfrac12\bigl(\lVert P_jX_i\rVert_2^2+
                         \lVert X_jP_i\rVert_2^2\bigr).
\]
After summing over $i,j$ and using \eqref{eq:PTP},
\[
 \left\lVert\sum_iX_i\right\rVert_2^2
 \leq\tfrac12\sum_i\left[
   \Tr(X_i^*TX_i)+\Tr(X_iTX_i^*)\right]
 \leq2\sum_i\lVert X_i\rVert_2^2.
\]
Thus $\lVert\mathcal A\rVert^2\leq2$.  Since
$\mathcal A^*(X)=(P_iXP_i)_i$, the same bound for $\mathcal A^*$ is exactly
\eqref{eq:hs-endpoint}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Interpolated pentagon inequality]\label{lem:pentagon-interp}
Let $s\in[1/2,1]$ and $p=1/s$.  For every faithful state $\sigma$ and every
pentagon packing,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pentagon-compression}
 \sum_i Z_s(\sigma,P_i)
 \leq B(p):=5^{(2-p)/2}2^{p-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map $\mathcal T:X\mapsto(P_iXP_i)_i$ has norm at most $\sqrt5$ from
$S_1$ to $\ell_1(S_1)$ by \eqref{eq:trace-endpoint}, and norm at most
$\sqrt2$ from $S_2$ to $\ell_2(S_2)$ by \eqref{eq:hs-endpoint}.  Complex
interpolation for Schatten direct sums \cite{BerghLofstrom1976,Bhatia1997}
gives, for $1\leq p\leq2$,
\[
 \left(\sum_i\lVert P_iXP_i\rVert_p^p\right)^{1/p}
 \leq 5^{(2-p)/(2p)}2^{(p-1)/p}\lVert X\rVert_p.
\]
One may obtain this directly from the three-lines function built from the
polar decompositions of $X$ and the dual block operator.  Put $X=\sigma^s$.
Then $\lVert X\rVert_p^p=\Tr\sigma=1$ and
$\lVert P_i\sigma^sP_i\rVert_p^p=Z_s(\sigma,P_i)$.  Raising the interpolated
bound to the $p$th power proves \eqref{eq:pentagon-compression}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pentagon bounds]\label{thm:pentagon}
Put $r=\alpha/(1-\alpha)$.  For $0\leq\alpha\leq1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pentagon-bounds}
 \sqrt5\left(\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\right)^r
 \leq\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)
 \leq\min\left\{\frac52,\sqrt5\,5^{r/2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $1/2\leq\alpha\leq2$,
$\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)=5/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The pentagon is vertex-transitive, so averaging gives
$1/\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)=\sup_{P,\sigma}\frac15\sum_iZ_{1-\alpha}(\sigma,P_i)$.
With $p=(1-\alpha)^{-1}$, \cref{lem:pentagon-interp} gives the lower bound
$5/B(p)$, which is the left side of \eqref{eq:pentagon-bounds}.

For the uniform upper bound $5/2$, use on $\mathbb C^5$ the commuting states
\[
 \rho_i=\tfrac12(\proj i+\proj{i+1}),\qquad \sigma=I/5.
\]
Their supports satisfy the pentagon nonedge relations, and every Petz order
has radius $\log_2(5/2)$.

For the other upper bound, let
\[
 u_i=\left(\cos\gamma,\sin\gamma\cos\frac{2\pi i}{5},
                    \sin\gamma\sin\frac{2\pi i}{5}\right),
 \qquad \cos^2\gamma=1/\sqrt5.
\]
Then $u_i\perp u_{i+2}$.  Against a faithful approximation to
$\proj c$, $c=(1,0,0)$, the pure-state Petz radius tends to
$\log_2\sqrt5/(1-\alpha)$, giving
$\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)\leq5^{1/(2-2\alpha)}$.

At $\alpha=1/2$, the lower bound is $5/2$.  Monotonicity in $\alpha$ and the
uniform upper bound give equality throughout $[1/2,2]$.
\end{proof}

In particular, $\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)>\sqrt5$ for every $\alpha>0$, while the
pure-handle upper bound is below $5/2$ for
\[
 \alpha<1-\frac{\log5}{2\log(5/2)}=0.121765\ldots.
\]

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:infinite}]
Let $r_k=8^{-(k+1)}$ and
$\alpha_k=r_k/(1+r_k)$.  By \cref{thm:pentagon},
\begin{align*}
 \vtheta_{\alpha_{k+1}}(C_5)&\leq\sqrt5\,5^{r_k/16},\\
 \vtheta_{\alpha_k}(C_5)&\geq\sqrt5(\sqrt5/2)^{r_k}.
\end{align*}
The integer inequality $2^{16}=65536<78125=5^7$ is equivalent to
$5^{1/16}<\sqrt5/2$, so each displayed upper bound is strictly below the
next displayed lower bound.  Also
$\vtheta_{\alpha_0}(C_5)\leq5^{9/16}<5/2$, and every value is strictly above
$\sqrt5$.  Hence all listed spectral points are distinct.
\end{proof}

Combining \cref{thm:infinite} with \cref{thm:small-graph} shows that
$\vtheta_0$, the countable ladder $(\vtheta_{\alpha_k})_k$, $\vtheta_1$,
and $\vtheta_2$ are pairwise distinct points of $X(\cG,\leq_*)$.

\section{A 32-vertex endpoint separation}\label{sec:small}

We now define the graph and state the exact finite certificate.  This section
is logically independent of \cref{thm:product}: it proves the finite strict
gap directly from the operational definitions \eqref{eq:cmin-def} and
\eqref{eq:sigma-def}.

\subsection{The magic-square seed and its clone graph}

Index the nine cells of a $3\times3$ array by $(i,j)\in\{0,1,2\}^2$.
There are six contexts: the three rows and three columns.  A vertex is a
context together with a sign assignment to its three cells.  Row assignments
have product $+1$; column assignments have products $(+1,+1,-1)$.  Each
context therefore has four vertices and the seed $F$ has 24 vertices.  Two
distinct vertices are declared orthogonal if they belong to the same context
or assign opposite signs to a shared cell.  This gives 216 ordered forbidden
pairs.

The standard two-qubit packing uses the Mermin--Peres array
\begin{equation}\label{eq:magic-square}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 Z\otimes I&I\otimes Z&Z\otimes Z\\
 I\otimes X&X\otimes I&X\otimes X\\
 Z\otimes X&X\otimes Z&Y\otimes Y
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For a context whose first two commuting observables are $A,B$, the vertex
with first two signs $(s,t)$ is represented by
\begin{equation}
 Q_{A,B;s,t}=\tfrac14(I+sA)(I+tB).
\end{equation}
These are rank-one projections.  Fix the first-row $(+,+)$ vertex $0$, so
$Q_0=\proj{00}$, and define $j_x=4\Tr Q_xQ_0$.  The four classes
$j=0,1,2,4$ have sizes $9,8,6,1$.

For a profile $k=(k_0,k_1,k_2,k_4)$, replace every class-$j$ vertex by
$k_j$ mutually nonadjacent clones and delete it if $k_j=0$.  The graph
\begin{equation}
 G_{32}=F[(2,1,1,0)]
\end{equation}
has $2\cdot9+8+6=32$ vertices.

\subsection{Gibbs and harmonic support weights}

For a faithful state $\sigma$ and a projection $P$, put
\begin{align}
 w_\sigma(P)&=\Tr\exp(P(\ln\sigma)P),\label{eq:gibbs-weight}\\
 h_\sigma(P)&=\Tr\sigma_{/P},\label{eq:harmonic-weight}
\end{align}
where $\sigma_{/P}$ is the largest positive operator below $\sigma$ whose
range is contained in $\ran P$.  These are exactly $Z_0(\sigma,P)$ and
$Z_{-1}(\sigma,P)$.

The certificate has two halves.  A rational direct sum of the packing
\eqref{eq:magic-square} and one-dimensional clique packings produces a Gibbs
vector and therefore a compatible cq channel with small information radius.
A moment-relaxation dual bounds every possible harmonic vector, in every
dimension, and therefore every common-dominator simulation.

\begin{proposition}[Direct operational bridge]\label{prop:operational-bridge}
Let $F[k]$ be a clone graph.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If a packing and state have Gibbs weights $w_x$, then
\[
 2^{C_{\min}(F[k])}\leq
 \left(\min_{x:k_x>0}\frac{w_x}{k_x}\right)^{-1}.
\]
\item Suppose every packing and state satisfy a linear harmonic inequality
$\sum_x\mu_xh_\sigma(P_x)\leq\lambda$, with $\mu_x\geq0$.  Then
\[
 \Sigma(F[k])\geq\frac{\sum_x\mu_xk_x}{\lambda}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the first part, split each parent support into $k_x$ clone supports using
a common finite diagonal refinement whose pieces have equal center mass.
On a support $P$, the Gibbs state
$\rho=\exp(P\ln\sigma P)/w_\sigma(P)$ satisfies
$D(\rho\Vert\sigma)=-\log_2w_\sigma(P)$.  The information-radius identity
therefore gives the bound.

For the second part, take an arbitrary feasible common-dominator tuple
$T\geq\rho_{x,a}$ and set $t=\Tr T$, $\sigma=T/t$.  Each
$\rho_{x,a}/t$ is feasible in the shorted-operator maximization, so
$h_\sigma(P_{x,a})\geq1/t$.  The clone supports are orthogonal.  If
$P_x=\sum_aP_{x,a}$, the trace of the shorted operator is superadditive over
these blocks; this follows from the Schur-complement formula for the diagonal
blocks of $(P_x\sigma^{-1}P_x)^{-1}$.  Hence
$h_\sigma(P_x)\geq k_x/t$.  The assumed inequality gives
$t\geq(\sum_x\mu_xk_x)/\lambda$.  Singular $T$ follows by faithful
regularization and a limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The dimension-independent moment relaxation}

Let $(P_x)$ be any packing and $\sigma>0$.  Set
$\Omega=\operatorname{vec}(\sqrt\sigma)$.  Let $F_x$ be the orthogonal
projection onto $\sigma^{-1/2}\ran P_x$ and set $E_x=F_x^{\mathsf T}$.

\begin{lemma}[Harmonic weight as a moment]\label{lem:harmonic-moment}
For every $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic-moment}
 (P_x\otimes E_x)\Omega=(I\otimes E_x)\Omega,\qquad
 \langle\Omega,I\otimes E_x\,\Omega\rangle=h_\sigma(P_x).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The range of $\sqrt\sigma F_x$ is $\ran P_x$, proving the vector identity.
Moreover $\sqrt\sigma F_x\sqrt\sigma$ is below $\sigma$ and supported in
$P_x$.  If $0\leq A\leq\sigma$ has range in $P_x$, then
$\sigma^{-1/2}A\sigma^{-1/2}$ is a contraction supported in $F_x$, hence is
at most $F_x$.  Thus $\sqrt\sigma F_x\sqrt\sigma=\sigma_{/P_x}$.  Taking
the vector-state expectation proves the second identity.
\end{proof}

The two projection families $P_x\otimes I$ and $I\otimes E_y$ commute, but
no orthogonality relation is imposed on the $E_y$.  Form a Gram matrix from
the 409 monomials
\[
 1,\quad (P_a\otimes I)_{a=0}^{23},\quad
 (I\otimes E_b)_{b=0}^{23},\quad
 (P_a\otimes E_b)_{a=b\text{ or }a\not\perp b}.
\]
Its entries are moments of words in the two families.  Idempotence, forbidden
adjacent $P$ pairs, and the two endpoint consequences of
\eqref{eq:harmonic-moment} give sound moment identifications.  Averaging over
the 48 seed automorphisms fixing vertex zero and taking real parts preserves
positive semidefiniteness.  The construction is a finite, symmetry-reduced
noncommutative moment relaxation in the spirit of
\cite{NPA2008,Mghirbi2026}.  The resulting table has 1009 moment keys.

Let $\Gamma(y)$ be the resulting real symmetric matrix, with one variable per
key.  The primal relaxation maximizes $t$ subject to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-primal}
 \Gamma(y)\succeq0,\qquad y_{1}=1,\qquad
 y_x\geq tk_x\quad(k_x>0).
\end{equation}
By \cref{lem:harmonic-moment}, every finite-dimensional packing and state
gives a feasible point.  Therefore the relaxation upper bounds the true
harmonic radius.

A dual certificate consists of $Z\succeq0$, multipliers $\mu_x\geq0$, and
$\lambda$ such that, for each moment key $\kappa$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-dual}
 \sum_{(i,j):\operatorname{key}(i,j)=\kappa}Z_{ij}
 +\sum_{x:\kappa_x=\kappa}\mu_x
 =\lambda\,[\kappa=1].
\end{equation}
Weak duality then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic-dual-bound}
 t_H(F[k])\leq\frac{\lambda}{\sum_x\mu_xk_x},
 \qquad
 \Sigma(F[k])\geq\frac{\sum_x\mu_xk_x}{\lambda}.
\end{equation}

\section{Certificate verification}\label{sec:certificate}

The file \texttt{certificates/small\_graph\_certificates.json} contains, for
each graph, the rational Gibbs mixture, the rational multipliers $\mu$, and
the nonzero orbits of the dual matrix $Z$.  The verifier performs the
following steps.

\begin{enumerate}
\item It rebuilds the 24-vertex seed, all 216 ordered forbidden pairs, the
two-qubit packing, the four overlap classes, and the 48 automorphisms.
\item It constructs the 409 monomials and the 1009-key moment table from the
sound rewrite rules used above.
\item It expands the orbit data, checks every affine equation
\eqref{eq:moment-dual} in rational arithmetic, checks $\mu\geq0$, and
recomputes the Gibbs witness exactly.
\item To prove $Z\succeq0$, it clears denominators and proposes an integer
matrix $L$ by rounding a floating-point Cholesky factor at scale $2^{40}$.
It then checks in exact integers that
\[
 R=2^{80}DZ-DLL^{\mathsf T}
\]
is symmetric diagonally dominant with nonnegative diagonal.  Hence
$R\succeq0$ and
$Z=LL^{\mathsf T}/2^{80}+R/(2^{80}D)\succeq0$.  Floating-point Cholesky
success is not an acceptance condition.
\end{enumerate}

The verifier has two independent negative controls.  One damages an affine
moment equation.  The other changes only entries attached to identically zero
moments, preserving every affine equation while breaking the exact positivity
test.  Both must be rejected.

\section{Discussion and open questions}

Theorem \ref{thm:spectrum} supplies infinitely many points of the
entanglement-assisted asymptotic spectrum beyond the Lov\'asz number.  It
does not alter the spectral upper bound on entanglement-assisted Shannon
capacity because every point in the present family is at least $\vtheta$.
Thus the possibility that $\vtheta$ is the least spectral point remains
open.

Several concrete questions remain.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does entanglement-assisted monotonicity extend beyond Petz order two?
The flagged-mixture proof cannot: it fails already on finite examples at
order three.
\item Is $\alpha\mapsto\vtheta_\alpha(C_5)$ continuous, and what is its exact
value on $(0,1/2)$?  Continuity would immediately turn the countable family
proved here into an uncountable one.
\item Does the limit as $\alpha\to\infty$ equal the complement of projective
rank?  Flat representations give the corresponding upper bound, but a
dimension-independent near-packing argument is missing.
\item How small can a graph with
$C_{\min}<\log_2\Sigma$ be?  The certificate proves existence on 32
vertices, not minimality.
\item Can the rational moment certificate be replaced by a short analytic
inequality, and is the first relaxation level exact for the three examples?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The certificate data and deterministic verification scripts accompany the
paper.  Their role is limited to the finite rational assertions in
\cref{sec:small,sec:certificate}.  The all-dimensional arguments in
\cref{sec:compression,sec:product,sec:union,sec:ea} are mathematical proofs,
not consequences of numerical tests.
